---
title: "Services"
description: "Reusable definitions of a workload's shape."
---

A **service** is a reusable definition of one workload's shape: which image,
which environment variables, which start command. It is declared once in
`uniac.yaml` and instantiated by [deployments](/concepts/deployments) — a
service is never directly deployable, and targeting one with `uniac plan` or
`uniac deploy` is an error.

```yaml
resources:
  worker:
    type: service
    image: "mendhak/http-https-echo:31"
    env:
      GREETING: hello
```

| Field | Required | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `image` | yes | OCI image reference. Prebuilt only — Uniac does not build from source. |
| `env` | no | Environment variables. Values may embed [references](/concepts/references). |
| `start_command` | no | Overrides the image CMD at the platform layer; the image is untouched. |

The full schema, including every rule enforced at plan time, is in
[The manifest](/concepts/manifest).

## No ports, no probes

A service declares no port. The project's internal network is
port-transparent: other services reach yours by instance name, on whatever
port your process binds. Which port that is travels through `env`, not through
the manifest. Liveness is observed on the container process, never by probing
a port — health endpoints, retries, and readiness are yours to build.

## Stateful services

`type: stateful` declares a service the platform caps at a single instance.
The body is identical to `type: service` — same three fields — and it is
instantiated by a deployment the same way. Use it for workloads where two
concurrent instances would corrupt state, such as a database:

```yaml
resources:
  db:
    type: stateful
    image: postgres:16
    env:
      POSTGRES_PASSWORD: "change-me"
```

`uniac status` reports such a service with `kind stateful`.

## The environment is exact

At container start the platform injects exactly the resolved declared `env` —
nothing more. The one fact the platform contributes is `${{self.host}}`, the
service's internal hostname, and only if you reference it. There are no
injected platform variables to discover, and nothing of Uniac runs inside your
container.
