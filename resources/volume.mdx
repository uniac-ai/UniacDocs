A volume is durable storage with a project-scoped identity. Its data and
lifetime are independent of the service bound to it.

## Required and optional configuration

A singleton service can declare one volume. The declaration requires:

| Field | Required | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `name` | Yes | Local volume name matching `^[a-z0-9]([a-z0-9-]{0,61}[a-z0-9])?$`. |
| `size_gb` | Yes | Size in GB when the volume is created: a number from 1 to 4096 after truncation toward zero. Later deployments declare the same size, as [Lifecycle](#lifecycle) describes. |
| `mount_path` | Yes | Absolute path other than `/`, with no `.` or `..` segments, repeated slashes, or trailing slash, outside `/proc`, `/sys` and `/dev`, and other than `/etc/resolv.conf`. |

The resulting project-scoped name is `<service>.<name>`, up to 127
characters, where `<service>` is the name of the deployment declaration that
instantiates the definition.

## YAML composition example

In [YAML composition](/composition/yaml), a volume declaration is a
mapping with `name`, `size_gb`, and `mount_path` inside the service definition's
optional `volumes` list. Omitting `volumes`, setting it to `null`, or using
`[]` declares no volume.

The singleton `cache` service mounts `cache.data` at `/data`. Redis writes its
append-only persistence files there, and the volume retains them across
container replacement.

```yaml
resources:
  cache-code:
    type: singleton
    image: redis:7-alpine
    start_command: redis-server --appendonly yes
    volumes:
      - name: data
        size_gb: 1
        mount_path: /data
  cache:
    type: deployment
    from: cache-code
```

In the generated description, the declaration is one `volume` object with
the scoped name, `size_gb`, and `mount_path`.

## Lifecycle

Declaring a new volume name provisions storage and binds the volume to the
service; declaring an existing volume that no service is bound to binds it.
A volume is bound to one service at a time. Redeploying with the same volume
name reuses its data, including when its mount path changes. Removing the
volume declaration on redeploy unbinds it; deleting the service also unbinds
its volume and preserves its data.

A volume keeps the size it was created with. A deployment that declares
another `size_gb` for an existing volume stops the singleton's running replica
and then fails; the service stays stopped until a deployment declares the
original size.

A fresh volume contains `lost+found`.

Deleting a volume requires name confirmation and destroys its data; it is
refused while a service is bound to the volume. The volume is removed once
its storage is released, and a deployment that declares the name of a
volume being deleted fails.

### States

| State | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `provisioning` | The volume's storage is being created. |
| `bound` | A service is bound to the volume. |
| `releasing` | No service is bound, and the volume's storage is still being released. |
| `available` | No service is bound, and the storage is released; the data is retained. |
| `deleting` | The deletion is accepted; the volume is removed once its storage is released. |
